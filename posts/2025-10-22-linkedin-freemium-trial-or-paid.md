# Should your product must be freemium, free-trial or only paid?

## Hook

## Post

> Should your product must be freemium, free-trial or only paid?
>
> Gaurav Vohra (ex: head of growth/product at Superhuman) lays out the best decision tree i found to guide this:
>
> Freemium (free access that may last forever):
>
> - There’s strong competitive pressure to offer a free tier (you risk losing market share if you charge) Claude
> - You’re building a product where network effects (value rising with more users) matter Dropbox
> - You’re gathering valuable data (for product improvement or monetisation) from free users Spotify/Claude again
>
> Downside of that “free” isn’t without cost - infrastructure, support, potential abuse, etc.
>
> Free Trial (time-/usage-limited free access):
>
> - You face competitive pressure but maybe not enough to warrant full freemium Lovable
> - You want to reach lower-intent customers who might convert if they experience the value first Wispr Flow
> - The value of your product is hard to convey via marketing alone, users must experience it to “get it” Attio
>
> Paid-Only - Sometimes the right choice is no free tier at all:
>
> - If you don’t face serious competitive pressure to go free.
> - If your product doesn’t need network effects (many users) or massive data collection.
> - If you find that users who pay up‐front treat the product more seriously and convert / retain better, as was the case with Superhuman.
>
> Most of the time it's legacy companies like Salesforce
>
