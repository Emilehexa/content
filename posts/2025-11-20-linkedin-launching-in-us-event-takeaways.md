# Most guidance on launching in the US misses the point.

## Hook

> Most guidance on launching in the US misses the point. Last Tuesday, we learned from two people who’ve actually done it (and well!).

## Post

> Most guidance on launching in the US misses the point. Last Tuesday, we learned from two people who’ve actually done it (and well!).
>
> Here’s what really matters:
>
> - PMF in Europe ≠ PMF in the US. You need to confront your product there as early as possible to really know where you stand.
>
> - You need at least $3M to launch a team today. It used to be $1M ten years ago.
>
> - If you’re solving a real pain and have a great product, you will find your first customers. Don’t overthink it.
>
> - Everything moves faster. Answer emails instantly. Follow up immediately. Speed is a competitive advantage.
>
> - What makes companies successful here is the “why not now?” mindset. Create momentum.
>
> - When something works, they go all in. No hesitation. They don’t overthink. They just ship. Even if it means breaking things (“Cloudflare down for 5 hours? OK.”).
>
> - You need to acclimate to US culture, not the other way around. If you don’t adapt, it won’t work.
>
> - Don’t act cheap. Pay top talent what they’re worth. The best can join OpenAI, Google, Anthropic - they won’t bet on your idea alone.
>
> - Relationships are transactional. People join because you pay well; you fire them when they underperform. Don’t be sentimental. It will kill your start-up.
>
> - Work in English inside your company early on. It creates a real international vibe and helps integrate US teams smoothly.
>
> - Top-tier US VCs will fund you if you have a killer team and product. Nothing else really matters.
>
> Well, now you know. What did we miss?
>
> Thanks a lot for coming Achkan & Adrien 🙏
