# You need to be contrarian to back non-obvious companies.

## Hook

> You need to be contrarian to back non-obvious companies.

## Post

> You need to be contrarian to back non-obvious companies.
>
> But are VCs shaped for that?
>
> Most of them don’t come from entrepreneurship. The path tends to be classic: a great school, then analyst, associate, principal, etc...
>
> Years of that give you a sharp eye for great deals, sometimes even for a non-obvious one.
>
> What’s usually missing is the ground experience to actually commit to it.
>
> And the incentives push the other way:
> - Your VC friends laugh at you for backing an unsexy company
> - Your brand suffers if your portfolio is full of names nobody knows
> - You’ll only prove them wrong years later, when you’ve probably moved on to something else
>
> What’s your incentive to be contrarian?
>
> So most end up fighting over the giga-obvious deals, at terms that might never return the fund.
>
> Great founders with less obvious businesses get left behind because the VCs didn’t want to risk their brand and money on them.
>
> Making those calls takes conviction, and that comes from having been on the ground. A great ex-founder or a real technical expert in your field can tell when an unsexy company is actually great, which makes the brand and money risk worth taking.
>
> How to escape the loop hole? How Tier2 VCs can make it when they either have no chances on top deals and no conviction on less obvious?
